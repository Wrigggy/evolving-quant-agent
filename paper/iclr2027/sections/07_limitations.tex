\section{Limitations}

Quantitative invariants are not complete specifications. A strategy can satisfy
temporal, accounting, and artifact checks while remaining economically useless
or failing another official property. Conversely, an invariant may be
inapplicable or underspecified by the public task. We therefore retain \NA{} and
\Unknown{} outcomes and keep official evaluation separate.

Repeated adaptation to optimization tasks creates selection overfitting; no
task that influences candidate selection is described as out of sample. The
current method does not train model weights and does not establish live-market
alpha, universal quantitative-research capability, or fully autonomous
experiment scheduling. Component interactions may also make individually useful
edits non-additive.
