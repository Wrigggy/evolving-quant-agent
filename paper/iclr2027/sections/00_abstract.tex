\begin{abstract}
Quantitative research agents are commonly improved by recursively refining the
factors, strategies, models, or programs they produce, while generic harness
optimization methods evolve the agent scaffold from trajectories and task
rewards. We study a domain-conditioned search representation for the latter.
We describe quantitative work through six general Research States spanning the
task mandate, evidence and data, quantitative representation, research
operation, evaluation and reconciliation, and artifact completion. We
introduce \method{}, in which an Evolver reconstructs the expected and observed
task-conditioned state from a Worker trajectory, selects a harness component
intended to mediate a specific state transition, and tests that intervention in
a fresh frozen-model \worker{}. Answer-free executable relations serve as
state-transition observations when applicable. The method records component
