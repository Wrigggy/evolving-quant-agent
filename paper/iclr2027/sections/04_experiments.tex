\section{Experimental Design}

We organize the prospective study around four questions.

\begin{description}
    \item[RQ1: Search yield.] Under matched public evidence and compute, does
    QRS-guided evolution produce more stable, protection-safe promotions than
    Generic trajectory-guided full-harness evolution?
    \item[RQ2: Mechanism.] At which stage---proposal, information-set review,
    Worker exposure or activation, state correction, target gain, repeat, or
    protection---do the two arms differ?
    \item[RQ3: Generalization.] Do the frozen final incumbents improve the
    complete official outcome vector on a no-feedback sealed panel?
    \item[RQ4: Efficiency.] What requests, tokens, verifier calls, cost, and
    wall time are required to reach the first stable promotion?
\end{description}

\subsection{Arms and Unit of Analysis}

The main QFBench comparison includes the frozen minimal Quant-H0 reference, a
matched Generic trajectory-guided full-harness Evolver, and the QRS-guided
full-harness Evolver. Generic and QRS use the same frozen base model, provider
route, initial harness, public evidence, mutation surface, family order,
budgets, admission checks, arm-blind Candidate Information-Set Reviewer,
Worker, verifier, repeat rule, and protection rule. QRS alone receives the
Quant Research State Card and state-, relation-, and component-conditioned
retrieval and routing. We reserve the name AHE for a separately audited faithful
reproduction rather than using it as a loose synonym for the Generic arm.

The statistical unit is an independent cumulative lineage. Each arm maintains
its own incumbent and never receives another arm's candidates or post-treatment
history. Families within a lineage are dependent because later candidates
inherit earlier promoted harness changes. We prefer at least four lineages per
arm with counterbalanced family order for a paper-level comparison. A two-per-
arm wave is reported only as a descriptive mechanism study.

\subsection{Task-Pool Disclosure and Evidence Splits}

Before the first main proposal, we freeze a task manifest containing the full
candidate pool, public inclusion and exclusion criteria, family strata, the
selection rule within each stratum, selected target/repeat/protection roles,
all exclusions and reasons, and the freeze time. Purposeful family selection is
allowed: the development panel may require a valid runtime, a publicly auditable
contract, nontrivial fresh Quant-H0 headroom, and an answer-free matched
protection task. We identify this as a deliberately stratified
mechanism-development panel, not an unselected sample of the full benchmark.

Any task, trajectory, score, or diagnostic that affects candidate generation,
refinement, promotion, or task selection is development evidence. A separate
family-stratified sealed panel is frozen before optimization from the remaining
eligible pool. Each final incumbent sees each sealed task once; sealed outcomes
do not return to the Evolver, Reviewer, retrieval index, promotion controller,
or task selector. QuantCodeEval is a later cross-benchmark check and is not
required to complete the first QFBench main comparison.

\subsection{Candidate and Promotion Protocol}

Each predeclared family-round permits one initial proposal and at most one
evidence-grounded refinement. A nonempty admitted candidate must receive
arm-blind Candidate Information-Set Review \texttt{PASS} with complete
cumulative-surface coverage before any fresh Worker. The target must strictly
improve the official outcome without introducing a failed property and must
resolve a nonempty property footprint. An independent repeat must reproduce
that footprint without a new failure. The answer-free protection candidate
must be aggregate-safe and its failed-property set must be a subset of the
matched parent set. Only then does the controller update the official incumbent.
Invalid runtime observations may receive only the separately frozen bounded
setup recovery; they are not scored as model or method failures.

\subsection{Primary Endpoints}

The development primary endpoint is the stable-promotion rate
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{SPR}
    = \frac{\sum_{\ell,f} \mathbb{1}[\text{Review pass, target gain,
    repeat-consistent, protection-safe}]}{N_{\mathrm{predeclared\ opportunities}}}.
\end{equation}
The denominator is fixed before execution. \texttt{ABSTAIN}, rejected
admission, Review \texttt{REJECT} or \texttt{INCONCLUSIVE}, no-gain target,
repeat inconsistency, and unsafe protection remain zero-valued opportunities;
they are not replaced by unreported best-of-$N$ attempts.

The final primary endpoint is the complete official task and property vector
for each frozen incumbent on the sealed panel. We report each task, the
equal-weight mean within each task family, and an equal-weight macro across
families. The family macro prevents a large family from dominating but never
replaces the per-task vector.

\subsection{Diagnostics, Efficiency, and Reporting}

The proposal, admission, Candidate Review, exposure or activation, Research
State correction, official target gain, repeat consistency, and protection
safety rates form a diagnostic funnel. These explain the primary endpoints and
are not individually called benchmark wins. We additionally report total model
requests, tokens, provider cost, verifier calls, and wall time per lineage and
to the first stable promotion. A lineage without a promotion is censored at and
charged its full frozen budget. Statistical comparisons, if sample size permits,
operate on paired independent-lineage outcomes; otherwise the paper reports
descriptive vectors and intervals without a significance or superiority claim.

\Boundary{The prospective main manifest, matched lineages, and sealed panel

Worker strict official gain; repeat and protection are additionally required
before calling that candidate stable.}
